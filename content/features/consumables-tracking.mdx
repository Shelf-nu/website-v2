# Consumables Tracking Software

Not everything you manage is a single, serialized asset. Cables, batteries, gloves, printer toner, lab reagents, PPE — these come in bulk, get used up, and need restocking. Shelf lets you track supplies and consumables by quantity **alongside** your individually-tracked assets, in one system, so your stock of disposables and your fleet of equipment live side by side.

![Two ways to track every item in Shelf: individually tracked items each get their own record and QR code, while quantity-tracked items use one record and one shared QR for a pool of identical units, with a count and a low-stock threshold.](/images/features/consumables/tracking-methods.svg)

---

## Two ways to track every item

When you create an item in Shelf, you choose how it's tracked:

- **Individually tracked** (the default): one record is one physical thing, with its own QR label, custody, and history. This is how you track laptops, cameras, power tools — anything serialized and unique.
- **Tracked by quantity**: one record is a *pool* of identical, interchangeable units sharing a single QR label. This is how you [track consumables by quantity](/glossary/quantity-tracked-assets) — the printer paper, the AA batteries, the box of nitrile gloves.

The tracking method is chosen at creation and is **permanent** — it locks once the item exists, so decide up front whether an item is a unique asset or a pool of stock. Quantity tracking complements individual tracking; it doesn't replace it.

Not sure which method fits an item? [How to Choose a Tracking Method](/knowledge-base/how-to-choose-a-tracking-method) walks the decision in four questions, including when a fleet of identical units belongs under an [Asset Model](/glossary/asset-models) instead.

---

## Track assets by quantity

A quantity-tracked item holds a **total quantity** in a **unit of measure** you define yourself — pieces, boxes, liters, kilograms, whatever fits. The whole pool shares **one QR label**, so a single scan finds the stock rather than hunting for one unit among hundreds.

Your **available** quantity is computed live as you go: the total, minus what's in custody, minus what's earmarked to a kit, minus what's checked out on an active booking. It shows what's genuinely on the shelf to hand out right now, without maintaining a separate "available" number by hand. Units reserved for future dates are counted separately, on their own **Reserved (bookings)** row, so a booking three weeks out never makes today's stock look smaller than it is.

---

## Consumable or returnable

Every quantity-tracked item has a **consumption type** that matches how it's actually used:

- **Used up**: a one-way consumable. Once it's issued, it's gone — paper, reagents, disposable gloves. Shelf applies that on both routes stock leaves by, a booking check-in and a custody hand-out, so the count drops either way.
- **Returnable**: a two-way pool. Units go out and come back, and a **consumption report** is recorded at check-in so you capture how much was actually used versus returned.

![Used up versus returnable consumption: used-up stock is consumed and not expected back, while returnable stock is checked out and reconciled at check-in into what returned versus what was used.](/images/features/consumables/consumption-types.svg)

For day-to-day stock changes, **quick adjustments** keep counts honest: restock to add stock (+), record a loss (−), or correct a miscount — each with an optional note. Every adjustment is logged with full attribution and shows up in the asset's activity **notes**, so there's a running audit trail of who changed what and why.

---

## Quantity-aware custody

Custody works at the unit level. Assign **any portion** of a pool to a person and let **multiple people each hold a share** of the same item at once — each custodian's quantity is tracked separately. Hand 5 of 20 radios to one crew lead and 10 to another, and Shelf knows exactly who's holding what.

Ending a hold follows the item's consumption type, so the stock figure stays honest without anyone remembering a rule. Returnable units go back into the available pool. A **used-up** consumable is marked as consumed instead, and those units come off the total for good. When only part of a hand-out was actually used, record that: a crew that took 40 pairs of gloves and used 10 hands the other 30 back in the same action. The split is written to the item's activity log, and a consume that crosses the minimum triggers the low-stock alert below.

---

## Low-stock alerts

Set a **minimum quantity** threshold on any quantity-tracked item and Shelf watches it for you. When a stock adjustment, a custody assignment, or a booking check-in takes available stock to or below that minimum, the **workspace owner and every admin** get an email alert, and an in-app notification goes to whoever made the change. When stock recovers, the same people get a **Back in stock** notice, and the **Low stock** badge on the asset page clears itself.

The alert fires **once per crossing**, not on every decrement. Shelf remembers that an item is already flagged and stays quiet until it recovers, so drawing a pool down over a week does not produce a week of email.

![Low-stock alert flow: when available stock drops to or below the minimum, Shelf emails the workspace owner and every admin and posts an in-app notification to whoever made the change. The alert fires once per crossing, and a Back in stock notice follows when the pool recovers.](/images/features/consumables/low-stock-alert.svg)

Alerts only fire when a minimum is set, so no threshold means no noise.

To see everything running low at once, open the [advanced asset index](/knowledge-base/advanced-asset-index-complete-guide), click **Filter**, and tick **Low stock only**. Add **Min quantity** as a column and each item's threshold sits next to its live count.

![The advanced asset index Filter popover, with the Low stock only checkbox described as "at or below reorder threshold".](https://qliecghuzfchfjwaisyx.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/website-images/knowledgebase/low-stock-filter.webp)

## Every stock change is on the record

Quick adjustments aren't the only thing tracked. Changing a count on the asset edit form, or changing the **min quantity** threshold itself, writes its own entry to the asset's **Activity** tab with the old and new value and who made the change, and the decrements a booking check-in or a consumable hand-out applies are logged the same way, with consumed and returned units recorded separately. However stock moved, it left a trail, so "who took 40 cables and when" has an answer without anyone keeping a side spreadsheet.

## Quantity in bookings & kits

Quantity-tracked stock plugs into the rest of Shelf:

- **Bookings**: reserve N units from the pool for a date range, and adjust that number as plans change. Shelf counts what's free on those dates and won't let a booking take units that aren't there. Because the count is date-aware, stock that comes back before a booking starts can be booked by someone else in the meantime, so the same 10 sandbags can serve three crews in three different weeks.
- **Kits**: include quantity-tracked stock in a kit, and the kit handles custody by quantity — so a kit that ships with 3 cables and 1 charger keeps its stock counts straight.

![Three booking timelines across three weeks. One camera booked across weeks one and two blocks a second overlapping booking. A pool of ten sandbags allows three separate seven-unit bookings in three different weeks, because never more than seven are needed at once. The same pool turns away an eight-unit booking in a week that already has a seven-unit booking, because only three units are free that week.](/images/features/consumables/booking-by-dates.svg)

Bookings — and quantity in bookings — are available on **Team plans**.

## Import & export

**CSV import/export** supports quantity tracking directly, with columns for quantity, unit of measure, min-quantity, and consumption type — so you can bring an existing supply list in (or pull it out) in bulk. Import/export is a premium capability.

---

## Asset models: fleets tracked unit by unit

**[Asset Models](/glossary/asset-models)** cover the middle ground between one-off assets and pooled stock: fleets of identical, serialized gear where every unit still needs its own record. Group individually tracked units under a model (say, "Dell Latitude 5550") and each unit keeps its own QR label, custody, and history, while new units created from the model inherit shared defaults like category and valuation. A model also carries one cover image: upload the product photo once and every unit that has no photo of its own displays it, across the asset index, reports, PDFs, and the mobile app. Bookings can [reserve by model](/features/bookings#book-by-model): reserve "4x HDMI cable" and scan any 4 units at pickup (bookings are on Team plans). Models group *individually tracked* assets only (a quantity-tracked pool cannot join a model), and units can join or leave a model at any time. Models themselves live under **Settings → Asset models**, and a fleet you already own is grouped from the asset index in one pass: select the units, then **Actions → Update asset model**.

## Available on every plan

Quantity tracking and asset models aren't plan-gated — they're available on **every Shelf plan**, including the free tier. The features that build on top keep their usual tiers: bookings (including reserving quantity and reserving by model) are on **Team plans**, and CSV import/export is premium. Shelf is open source and free to start, with the free tier supporting a single user.

## Common use cases

- **IT supply closet**: track cables, adapters, batteries, and toner by count. Set a min-quantity on the essentials so the workspace owner gets an email before you run out.
- **Lab and medical supplies**: manage reagents, gloves, and consumables in their real units (liters, boxes, pieces), with returnable items reporting actual usage at check-in.
- **Job-site consumables and PPE**: hand portions of bulk PPE to different crews, track who's holding what, and restock from the field.
- **Event materials**: reserve N of a printed program or giveaway for a booking, scan-to-assign at checkout, and reconcile what came back.
