Audits let you verify that what's physically in a location matches what your records say should be there. Shelf flags what was **found**, what's **missing**, and anything **unexpected** that showed up. When the audit is done, export a PDF report.

Audits is a paid add-on available on any Shelf plan. It includes a **7-day free trial** so you can test it on your own inventory before committing. This guide walks you through your first audit — start to finish — in about 20 minutes.

---

## Before You Start

You need three things in place:

1. **Assets in Shelf** — at least a few items in the location you plan to audit. If your inventory is empty, [add some assets first](/knowledge-base/adding-new-assets).
2. **QR labels on those assets** — the audit scan page reads QR codes to confirm physical presence. If you haven't printed labels yet, see [Using Shelf QR Codes with Avery Label Sheets](/knowledge-base/using-shelf-qr-codes-with-avery-label-sheets) or [Printing Custom Branded QR Code Labels](/knowledge-base/how-to-print-custom-branded-qr-code-labels-with-shelf).
3. **Audits activated** — from the sidebar, click **Audits**. If you haven't activated the add-on yet, Shelf shows an unlock page. Only the **workspace owner** can start the trial or subscribe, since it is the owner's card the add-on bills to and each workspace gets the free trial once. Administrators and everyone else see "Contact your workspace owner to enable the Audits add-on for your organization" instead of the buttons. No credit card is required to start the trial.

## Step 1: Create an Audit Session

1. Click **Audits** in the sidebar
2. Click **New Audit** (top-right)
3. Fill in:
   - **Name** — something descriptive for your team (e.g. "Studio B Equipment Check", "Server Room Q2 2026")
   - **Description** *(optional)* — instructions for the person doing the scan (e.g. "Scan each asset and provide a condition photo")
   - **Due date** *(optional)* — when the audit should be completed by
4. **Select which assets to include** — you can scope by location, category, tag, or any combination. Shelf builds the "expected" asset list from your current records.
5. **Assign the audit** *(optional)* — pick a team member who'll do the scanning. Admins and owners can perform any audit in the workspace whether or not it is assigned, so assigning is how you hand the job to a **self-service** or **base** user. It also tells everyone else whose job it is.
6. Click **Save** to create the session

> **Shortcut: start an audit from your Locations or Kits.** Instead of building the scope inside the dialog, you can kick off an audit straight from a list page. On the **Locations** page (or the **Kits** page), tick one or more rows, open the **Actions** dropdown, and choose **Create audit**. Shelf creates a single audit covering the union of every asset across the selected locations or kits — ideal for a room-by-room sweep or for confirming kit contents are complete. If you select all with a search or filter active, only the matching rows are included. You'll still name the audit and (optionally) assign it before it's created.

## Step 2: Open the Audit Overview

Click into the audit you just created. The **Overview** tab shows:

- **Statistics** — Expected / Found / Not scanned / Unexpected counts. On a fresh audit, everything sits under **Not scanned**, because nothing has been counted yet
- **Audit Information** — status, created date, assignee, due date
- **Findings** — the notes and photos people record as they go, grouped by the asset each one is about. On a fresh audit it reads *Nothing was recorded during this audit*
- **Asset list** — every asset Shelf expects to find, each with a **Not scanned** chip until someone reaches it

This is your live dashboard. As scanning progresses, the counters update in real time.

![Audit overview with Expected 20, Found 0, Not scanned 20, ready to scan](https://qliecghuzfchfjwaisyx.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/website-images/features/audits-overview-pending.webp)

Note what a new audit does **not** say. Nothing is called missing yet, because nobody has looked. Shelf saves that word for the moment you complete the audit.

## Step 3: Start Scanning

Click **Start scanning** (in the header) to open the dedicated scan page. This page is purpose-built for audits — separate from the regular QR Scanner — so there's no confusion about which workflow you're in.

Two ways to register assets:

- **Scanner mode** — focus the text field and use a handheld barcode scanner to scan QR labels rapid-fire
- **Camera mode** — toggle to **Camera** in the top-right and use your phone or laptop camera to scan the QR

Each scan registers the asset as **Found**. The progress bar updates live: "Audit: Studio B (Equipment Check) • 3/19 found".

You can also **Add comment** or **Add image** per asset during the scan — useful for documenting condition, damage, or wrong labels.

Those two buttons sit on assets you have **not** scanned yet as well as on the ones you have. That is deliberate: the empty shelf where the camera should have been is worth photographing, and the note belongs on the audit whether or not the item ever turned up.

![Audit scan page with the progress bar and each scanned row marked Expected or Unexpected](https://qliecghuzfchfjwaisyx.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/website-images/features/audits-scan.webp)

### Undoing a Scan

Scan the wrong label and you can take it straight back off. Every scanned row on the scan page carries a **bin icon** at the end of it, and clicking it removes that scan from the audit.

What happens next depends on what you scanned:

- **An asset that was on the expected list** goes back to **Not scanned**, and the time of the scan and the name of the person who made it are cleared with it. It is as though nobody had reached that shelf yet.
- **An asset that was not on the list** leaves the audit entirely. Nothing but the scan put it there, so removing the scan removes the row.

The Found, Not scanned and Unexpected counts are then recounted from the rows themselves rather than nudged down by one, so the totals can never drift away from the list they summarise. The **Activity** tab records who removed which scan.

You are not limited to the scans you made in this sitting. Reopening the scan page restores everything already recorded on the audit into the list, so a mis-scan from yesterday morning is as removable as one from a minute ago.

Removal only works while the audit is still running. Once it is **Completed** or **Cancelled** its numbers have been reported, and Shelf refuses the change with *"This audit is no longer live, so its scans cannot be changed."*

#### Undoing a Scan in the Companion App

From [Shelf Companion](/knowledge-base/shelf-ios-companion-getting-started) **1.5.0**, the same correction is available on the phone, where a mis-scan is most likely to happen in the first place.

1. On the audit's scanned list, **tap the row** you want to take back. The sheet that holds its notes and photos opens.
2. Scroll to the foot of the sheet and tap **Remove scan**.
3. Confirm. The app asks *"Remove this scan?"* and explains that the asset *"goes back to how it was before you scanned it. You can scan it again."*

The outcome matches the web exactly: an expected asset returns to **Not scanned**, an unexpected one leaves the audit, and the counts recount from the rows.

When the row carries evidence, the sheet adds *"Your notes and photos stay on the audit."* That is literally true. Evidence is attached to the audit's row for the asset rather than to the scan, so undoing a mis-scan never costs you the photo you took, and the evidence is still there if you scan the asset again.

A **Completed** or **Cancelled** audit refuses the removal on the phone for the same reason it does on the web.

#### What a Scanned Row Says on the Phone

Also from 1.5.0, a scanned row's second line answers **where the asset is** instead of repeating the word *Found*, which every row on that list already was:

- **An asset the audit expected** shows its **location**, so a walk through a room tells you which of the found items are not where your records say they should be.
- **An asset the audit did not expect** reads **Not on this audit**, with its location after it when it has one. For that row, not belonging here is the useful fact.

The count of unexpected assets at the top of the screen is **tappable**: it filters the scanned list to just those, and tapping again restores the rest. Notes and photos are counted separately on each row, so one note plus one photo never reads the same as two photos.

**Archived audits are reachable from the app**, with an **Archived** filter, and the **All** filter includes them. An audits list you were used to on the phone will get longer.

### If an Asset Is Deleted After It Was Scanned

An audit is a historical record, so a scan keeps its meaning even after the asset behind it is gone. Every scan stores the asset's name and whether the audit expected it, at the moment it is scanned.

Delete that asset later and the scan page still shows the row, named **"Camera body A (deleted)"**, still counted as found, and still badged **Expected** if the audit had expected it. The row becomes read-only, since there is no asset left to open, comment on or photograph. A scan recorded before Shelf began storing names, whose asset has since gone, reads **"Deleted asset"** with the code that was scanned.

A live asset always shows its **current** name, not the stored one, so renaming an asset mid-audit is reflected straight away. The stored name is there to survive deletion, not to freeze naming.

One thing to know: the asset table on the audit's **overview** lists the assets the audit is scoped to, and a deleted asset is no longer one of them, so it has no row there while the audit's counts still include it. The scan page is where the full scan history lives.

## Step 4: Review Results

Go back to the **Overview** tab at any point to check progress. Every asset carries one of these:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Found** | Scanned and matches the expected list |
| **Not scanned** | On the expected list, nobody has reached it yet. This is what you see for the whole of a running audit |
| **Unexpected** | Scanned but NOT on the expected list — the item has moved from another location, or was never recorded |
| **Missing** | What **Not scanned** turns into the moment you complete the audit: expected, never scanned, and now on the record as such |

**Missing only appears after you complete the audit.** Until then an unscanned asset is simply one you have not got to, so the statistics tile, the asset rows, the audits list, and the PDF receipt all read **Not scanned** instead. That means a count you started this morning never tells you half your inventory is missing, and when the word does appear it is a finding rather than a state of play.

Use the **Filter by audit status** dropdown to isolate items for follow-up. The filter follows the same rule: it offers **Not scanned assets** while the audit is open and **Missing assets** once it is completed.

## Step 5: Complete the Audit

When scanning is done:

1. Click **Complete Audit** (bottom-right of the scan page, or from the Actions dropdown on the overview)
2. Check the **Audit Summary** in the confirmation dialog. It states the Expected, Found, Missing and Unexpected counts one last time, and completing cannot be undone
3. Add a **Completion Note** and up to five photos if you have closing observations. Both are optional, and both print in the receipt's Findings section
4. Confirm with **Complete Audit**. Shelf sends a **summary email** to the audit creator with final counts
5. From the completed audit's overview, click **Actions → Download Receipt** to export a **PDF report** with found/missing/unexpected breakdowns

The PDF is your compliance artifact — hand it to insurance, your manager, or file it for regulatory review. It carries a **Findings** section as well as the counts: your completion note and its photos first, then one block per asset holding what was written about that item next to the pictures taken of it.

### Completing an Audit Where Nothing Was Found

An audit that turns up nothing is still a finding, and often an urgent one: the cupboard is empty, the van was cleared out, the room was cleaned before anyone counted it. You can complete it.

The confirmation dialog says what that decision means before anything is written:

> No assets were scanned. Completing now marks all 20 expected assets as missing.

Confirm and every expected asset is recorded as **Missing**, exactly as an asset you scanned past would have been. The receipt, the counts and the audits list all read the same way they would after any other completion.

## After the Trial

The 7-day trial gives you unlimited audits. When it expires:

- **Completed audits stay visible** — you keep full access to results, reports, and PDFs from audits run during the trial
- **New audits require the paid add-on** — activate from Audits in the sidebar; pricing is managed through your Shelf account
- **No data lost** — nothing is deleted when the trial ends

If you found value during the trial, the add-on is available on every Shelf plan (Free, Plus, Team, Enterprise).

## Tips for a Successful First Audit

- **Start small**: audit one room or one category, not the entire inventory. Prove the workflow before scaling.
- **Print test labels first**: run a [test print](/knowledge-base/using-shelf-qr-codes-with-avery-label-sheets#3-drop-in-the-pngs-and-print-then-test) and scan several codes before committing a full batch.
- **Assign the audit to yourself** for your first run — you'll understand the scan flow before delegating to someone else.
- **Use the camera toggle on your phone** if you don't have a handheld scanner. It works with any phone camera, no app needed.
- **Add photos during the scan** for anything that looks damaged, misplaced, or mislabelled — it saves a second pass later.

## FAQ

### Who can perform an audit?

- **Owners and administrators** can scan, add notes and photos, and complete **any** audit in their workspace, assigned or not. Assigning an audit to someone else does not lock an admin out of it.
- **Self-service and base users** can only work on audits they have been assigned to. Everything else in the audit list is out of reach for them.

The rule is the same on the web and in the [Shelf Companion](/mobile-app) app, so an action that works in the browser works on the phone too.

### Where do the notes and photos end up?

On the audit's **Overview** tab, in the **Findings** column on the right.

**About this audit** comes first: the completion note and any photos of the count as a whole. Under it, one card per asset somebody recorded something about, with the note, who wrote it, when, and the photographs of that same item together.

The asset table below points you at them. Every row that carries something shows a small chip counting the notes and the photos separately, and clicking it opens that asset's notes and images. On a long list that chip is how you tell the one item worth following up from the rest.

Findings holds what **people** recorded. Shelf's own trail (statuses changing, the audit being started and completed) stays on the **Activity** tab, and that tab is where the note log downloads as CSV.

![One asset's Findings card, with the condition note above the photographs taken of the same item](https://qliecghuzfchfjwaisyx.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/website-images/features/audit-findings-asset.webp)

### Can I record a note on something I could not find?

Yes, and it is one of the more useful things you can do. **Add comment** and **Add image** appear on rows that are still **Not scanned**, so you can photograph the empty slot or write down what the storeroom manager told you. The note stays on the audit record and prints in the receipt's **Findings** section, whether or not that asset was ever scanned.

### I scanned the wrong thing. Can I take it back?

Yes, while the audit is still running. Click the **bin icon** at the end of the scanned row on the scan page. An expected asset returns to **Not scanned** with the scan time and scanner cleared; an asset that was not on the list disappears from the audit altogether. The counts are recounted from the rows, and the **Activity** tab keeps a record of who removed what.

Reopening the scan page restores every scan already on the audit, so this is not limited to the ones you made in this sitting. A **Completed** or **Cancelled** audit refuses the change: its numbers have already been reported.

### Can I complete an audit if I found nothing at all?

Yes, and Shelf tells you what that means first. The confirmation dialog says *"No assets were scanned. Completing now marks all N expected assets as missing."* Confirm and every expected asset is recorded as **Missing**. An empty cupboard is a finding worth putting on the record, not a reason to leave the audit hanging.

### What happens to an audit if I delete an asset that was already scanned?

The scan stays. Its row on the scan page keeps the name the asset had when it was scanned, marked **(deleted)**, and it still counts toward the audit's found total and still shows whether the audit expected it. The row is read-only from then on, because there is no asset page left behind it. The asset table on the audit's overview lists the assets the audit is scoped to, so a deleted asset has no row there.

### Can multiple people scan the same audit?

Currently, each audit session supports a single assigned scanner. For large-scale audits, create multiple sessions scoped to different areas and assign each to a different team member.

### Can I create an audit from the Locations or Kits page?

Yes. You don't have to build the scope inside the **New Audit** dialog. On the **Locations** page or the **Kits** page, select one or more rows, open the **Actions** dropdown, and choose **Create audit**. Shelf creates one audit covering the union of every asset across the selected locations or kits. Selecting all with a search or filter applied audits only the matching rows. This is the fastest way to start a multi-location or multi-kit audit, and it requires the same permission as the **New Audit** button — so anyone who can already create audits can use it.

### Why does my audit say "Not scanned" instead of "Missing"?

Because the count is not finished. An expected asset nobody has reached yet reads **Not scanned** for as long as the audit is open. Complete the audit and everything still unscanned becomes **Missing**, on the statistics tile, on the asset rows, in the audits list, and on the PDF receipt you download.

### What happens to assets marked "Missing"?

Nothing is automatically changed in your Shelf database. Missing means "expected, and never scanned before the audit closed" — you decide what action to take: follow up with the last known custodian, mark as lost, or investigate.

### Can I re-open a completed audit?

No. Completed audits are final — their results, statuses, and PDF receipt are preserved exactly as they were when the audit closed.

If you want to re-run the same scope, **duplicate the audit instead** of starting from scratch. Admins and owners can open any Completed, Cancelled, or Archived audit, click **Actions → Duplicate audit**, and Shelf creates a fresh Pending session with the same asset list, name (with a "(Copy)" suffix), and description. No notes, scans, or assignee carry over — the duplicate is ready to scan. See [Duplicate an Audit](/knowledge-base/duplicate-audit) for full instructions.

### Can I cancel an audit?

Yes. The audit's creator can cancel their own audit at any time before it reaches **Completed**, **Cancelled**, or **Archived**. Owners and admins can also cancel any audit in the workspace — including audits created by other team members — using the same **Actions → Cancel** menu. Self-service and base users can only cancel audits they created themselves.

### Can I archive old audits?

Yes. Admins and owners can archive any Completed or Cancelled audit via **Actions → Archive**. Archived audits are removed from the default list but stay accessible via the status filter.

### Can I permanently delete an audit?

Yes. Admins and owners can permanently delete any **Archived** audit — archiving must happen first as a safety step. Open the archived audit, click **Actions → Delete**, and type the audit name to confirm. To delete multiple audits at once, filter the list by **Archived**, select them, and choose **Delete** from the Actions dropdown (type DELETE to confirm). See [Permanently Delete Archived Audits](/knowledge-base/delete-audits) for full instructions.
