Not everything you manage is a single, uniquely-identifiable item. Laptops, cameras, and vehicles each deserve their own record — but pens, USB-C cables, gloves, and other supplies are managed by **how many you have**, not by individual identity. Shelf now supports both. When you create an asset you choose a **tracking method**, and quantity-tracked assets let one record stand for a pool of identical units at a location.

---

## Two tracking methods

When you add or edit an asset, you pick how it should be tracked:

- **Individually tracked** *(default)* — One record equals one physical item, exactly as Shelf has always worked. Each item has its own QR code, custody history, and booking history.
- **Tracked by quantity** — One record represents a pool of identical units (for example, 500 cable ties). You manage the count rather than each unit, and a single QR code represents the whole pool.

| | Individually tracked | Tracked by quantity |
|---|---|---|
| **QR code** | One per item | One shared QR for the pool |
| **Custody** | One custodian per item | Several people can each hold a portion |
| **Bookings** | Reserve specific items | Reserve a quantity from the pool |
| **History** | Full per-unit lifecycle | Aggregate quantity changes |

---

## Creating a quantity-tracked asset

1. Click **Assets** in the sidebar, then **New asset**
2. Give the asset a **name**
3. Under **Tracking method**, choose **Tracked by quantity**
4. Fill in the quantity fields that appear:
   - **Quantity** — how many units you have (for example, 500)
   - **Unit of measure** — what they're counted in (for example, "pcs", "boxes", "liters")
   - **Min quantity** *(optional)* — the low-stock threshold; Shelf alerts you when available stock drops to or below this number
   - **Consumption type** — how the stock behaves after checkout (see [Consumption types](#consumption-types) below)
5. Add a **Category**, **Tags**, **Location**, and any other fields as usual
6. Click **Save**

## Consumption types

Quantity-tracked assets can behave in one of two ways after they're handed out or checked out:

- **Used up (one-way)** — Units are consumed and not expected back. Checking out 50 gloves permanently reduces the count by 50. Best for disposables: gloves, masks, single-use kits.
- **Returnable (two-way)** — Units are checked out and returned, with a consumption report on the way back. Check out 10 cables; when they come back you report what returned and what was lost or used up. Returned units go back into available stock and the rest are deducted. Best for reusable supplies that occasionally go missing.

When new stock arrives, you can **restock** a quantity-tracked asset to increase its count. Every quantity change is logged for an audit trail.

## Quantity-aware custody

For quantity-tracked assets, custody is by portion rather than all-or-nothing:

- Assign part of the pool to a person — for example, "20 of 100 USB-C cables to Sarah." The available count drops by 20.
- Several team members can hold different quantities of the same asset at once.
- Releasing custody — fully or partially — returns those units to the available pool.

---

## Where the units are

A pooled asset doesn't have to sit in one place. Its asset page lists every **placement**, meaning a location plus how many units are there, so 100 USB-C cables can be recorded as 60 in the main stockroom and 40 in the van. Each placement has its own row you can adjust, and any units you haven't placed yet show as unplaced.

Placements come from two directions:

- **Manual placements** are the ones you set yourself. You can move units between locations from the placement row.
- **Kit placements** appear when the asset is inside a [kit that has a location](/knowledge-base/kits#kit-location). The kit holds a slice of the pool, say 20 of the 100 cables, and those units follow the kit. They're marked **via kit** on the asset page; to move them, change the kit's location rather than editing the placement. Change the kit's quantity and the slice at its location follows.

The two are independent, so a pool can be partly placed by hand and partly held by kits at the same time. Take the asset out of the kit and the kit's slice is released back to unplaced.

## On the mobile app

Quantity-tracked assets are available in the [Shelf Companion](/mobile-app) app for iPhone and Android, so you can read stock and hand out units from the floor:

- The **assets list** shows a quantity pill on each pooled asset's row.
- The **asset detail** screen shows a quantity card with the total plus a per-status breakdown — available, in custody, reserved, and checked out — and a custody row for each person, listing how many units they currently hold.
- Tap **Assign** to give a set number of units to a team member. The amount is capped at the units actually available to assign.
- Tap a holder's custody row to **release** units back to the pool. The amount is capped at the units that person can release.
- On a booking, check out part of a pooled line and leave the rest booked, then record the return with the same **Returned / Lost / Damaged** split (or **Consumed / Lost / Damaged** for consumables) the web check-in drawer uses.

Individually tracked assets look and behave exactly as before in the app. See [Getting Started with Shelf Companion](/knowledge-base/shelf-ios-companion-getting-started) for the full mobile walkthrough.

## Quantity in bookings

You can reserve a quantity of a pooled asset for a booking. Two different numbers matter here, and Shelf keeps them apart on purpose.

### On the asset page: what's on the shelf right now

The **Quantity Overview** card's **Available** row is the physical count: the total, minus units held in custody, minus units earmarked to a kit, minus units checked out on an active booking. Reservations for future dates never pull this number down. They get their own **Reserved (bookings)** row, whose tooltip tells you how many units are spoken for and across how many upcoming bookings. Placements at locations are listed too, on an **In locations** row, and they don't reduce availability: a unit can sit in Studio A and still be free to book.

![The Quantity Overview card on a quantity-tracked asset, showing total quantity, available, in locations, in custody, unit of measure, min quantity and consumption behavior](https://qliecghuzfchfjwaisyx.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/website-images/knowledgebase/quantity-overview-card.webp)

### Inside a booking: what's free for those dates

Availability in a booking is calculated for that booking's date range. Shelf takes the pool (the total, less custody and kit allocations) and subtracts the **highest number of units reserved at any single moment** inside your window. Bookings whose dates don't overlap yours never compete with you: a pool of 10 with three separate 7-unit bookings in three separate weeks still has 3 units free for each of those weeks, because only 7 are ever needed at once.

Overdue bookings are the exception. Until an overdue booking is checked in, there's no date at which its units come back, so they keep counting against every window.

The **Adjust booked quantity** dialog states the windowed figure directly, for example *"4 of 12 sandbags available"* for your dates, along with how many units other bookings have reserved. If your role can see other people's bookings, a **View bookings** link takes you to that asset's booking list.

### When a pool is over-committed

Shelf surfaces over-commitment rather than letting it sit:

- An amber **Stock conflict** pill on any bookings list (the Bookings index, an asset's or kit's **Bookings** tab, **My bookings**, and a team member's bookings) marks a booking whose quantity-tracked assets are over-reserved for its dates.
- A red **Insufficient stock** badge on the asset's row inside the booking, when that booking reserves more units than the pool can supply.
- An amber **Checked out elsewhere** badge when the units fit your dates but some are physically out right now on another booking. This one is informational, not a blocker: those units are due back before your booking starts.

**Reducing** the quantity on an over-reserved booking is always allowed, so an over-commitment can be unwound without cancelling the booking. Only increases beyond what the window can supply are refused, with a message naming the shortfall: *"Only 3 of 10 units available in this window — reduce the quantity to continue."*

The pool is protected from the other direction too. Lowering an asset's total quantity below what's already committed to custody, kits, or overlapping bookings is blocked, so stock can't silently disappear out from under a booking that depends on it.

### Coming back

For returnable assets, check-in reconciles what came back versus what was consumed before the booking closes. That reconciliation runs on the web and in the Companion app, so a partial return can be settled on the floor.

## Total value counts every unit

The value you set on an asset is the **per-unit** value. For a quantity-tracked asset, Shelf multiplies it by the quantity on hand to get the pool's total worth — a box of 100 cables at €2 each counts as €200, not €2. That total flows through everywhere value is summed: the dashboard inventory-value tile, location and kit totals, a booking's total asset value (including the pull-list PDF), the value-based reports, and CSV exports. Individually tracked assets are unaffected — with a quantity of one, per-unit and total are the same number.

## Low-stock alerts

If you set a **min quantity**, Shelf watches the available count for that asset. When a stock adjustment or custody assignment takes the count to or below the threshold, the workspace owner is notified by email so you can reorder before you run out. The **Low stock** badge on the asset page clears automatically when stock rises back above the threshold through a restock or returns.

## Asset models (optional)

You can group individually tracked assets under an **[Asset Model](/glossary/asset-models)** (for example, "Dell Latitude 5550") so that shared defaults — like category and valuation — are pre-filled when you create new assets from that model. Asset models are managed under **Settings → Asset models**, and you can bulk-create assets from a model. Models also unlock [booking by model](/features/bookings#book-by-model): reserve "4x" of a model and scan any 4 units at pickup. Note that models group individually tracked assets only; a quantity-tracked pool cannot join a model.

## When to use which method

- Use **Individually tracked** when each unit needs its own QR code, custody chain, and history — laptops, cameras, vehicles, instruments.
- Use **Tracked by quantity** when only the count matters — cables, adapters, gloves, fasteners, and other supplies.

For the full decision framework, including when a fleet of identical units belongs under an Asset Model, see [How to Choose a Tracking Method](/knowledge-base/how-to-choose-a-tracking-method).

> **Need per-unit history on a pooled item?** Printing several QR labels for one quantity-tracked asset still points every label at the same pool — they all show and adjust the same count. If you need independent history per unit, track those items individually instead.
