When a piece of equipment is broken, the person holding it is usually the first to know. **Report a problem** lets every member of a workspace say so from the asset's own page: they type what is wrong, press **Send**, and the people who run the workspace hear about it.

Each report does two things:

- It is saved as a line in the asset's history, with the reporter's name and their words.
- It is emailed to the owners, administrators and managers of the workspace.

Every role can send one: Owner, Administrator, Manager, Self-service and Base. Base and Self-service users cannot write notes on an asset, so this is their way to pass a problem on.

## Send a Report

1. Open the asset. Scanning its QR label with your phone camera opens the same page in the browser.
2. Open the **Actions** menu at the top of the page and choose **Report a problem**. A Base user sees only this item in the menu.
3. Under **What is wrong?**, describe the problem, for example: the battery does not charge.
4. Press **Send**.

![The Report a problem dialog on an asset page, with the What is wrong? field, a 1,000 character counter, and the Cancel and Send buttons](https://qliecghuzfchfjwaisyx.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/website-images/knowledgebase/report-a-problem-dialog.webp)

The dialog then reads **Report sent** and says where the report went: *"Your report is saved in the asset's history and is being emailed to the owners, administrators and managers of this workspace."* It stays open until you press **Close**, so you have a clear sign that it arrived.

A report is between 3 and 1,000 characters of plain text. It cannot carry a photo.

## What the Workspace Gets

### A line on the asset

The asset's **Activity** tab shows the reporter's name as a link, followed by *reported a problem:* and their text. Owners, Administrators and Managers can open that tab. The line is also part of the asset's activity export. Formatting typed into a report is shown as plain text, so a report cannot place a live link or an image in the asset's history.

### An email

The subject is **Problem reported:** followed by the asset's name. The email names the workspace, the asset and the reporter, quotes what they wrote and has a **View asset** button. Replying to it answers the reporter directly, so a quick question back needs no extra step.

Who receives it:

- Every Owner, Administrator and Manager of the workspace.
- Not the reporter. An administrator who reports a problem does not get their own report by email.
- If nobody else holds one of those roles, the report is still saved, and the dialog says *"No email was sent."*

## Rules Worth Knowing

- Team workspaces only. A Personal workspace has one person, so there is nobody to report to, and **Report a problem** is not in the menu there.
- You can report an asset whose page opens for you, including one that is not available to book. This is deliberate: someone who scans a broken item's label lands on that page.
- Not on an archived asset. The menu item is greyed out while an asset is archived. Reinstate the asset first.
- One member can send 10 reports per workspace in an hour, because every report emails several people. After that the dialog says *"You have sent 10 problem reports in the last hour. Try again later."*
- A report does not change the asset. It does not mark the asset unavailable, block it from bookings or open a ticket, and there is no list of open reports or a "resolved" tick. To take a broken item out of circulation, someone who can edit the asset switches off **Available for bookings** on it, or [archives it](/knowledge-base/archive-and-reinstate-assets). To schedule the repair, set an [asset reminder](/knowledge-base/asset-reminders).
- Reports are sent from the web app today. The Shelf Companion app has no Report a problem screen yet. On a phone, open the asset in the browser.
- A kit page has no Report a problem; report the asset inside the kit that has the fault.
