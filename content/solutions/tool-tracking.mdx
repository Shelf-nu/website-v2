# Tool Tracking

**Tool tracking software** is a system that records which tools you own, who has each one and where it is, on a job site, in a truck or back in the tool crib, and logs every hand-off so a missing tool can be traced to the last person who had it.

![Tool tracking overview with field equipment management](https://qliecghuzfchfjwaisyx.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/website-images/solutions/main-tool-tracking.webp)

---

## What Is Tool Tracking?

Tool tracking is the practice of monitoring who has which tools, where they're located, and when they move between sites, trucks, or storage. It ensures accountability across crews, reduces loss, and keeps operations running smoothly.

Modern tool tracking goes beyond static lists. It supports dynamic field workflows: technicians scanning tools onto trucks, supervisors checking inventory across sites, and crews handing off equipment during shift changes—all with instant visibility.

---

## Why Teams Need Modern Tool Tracking

Field teams face persistent challenges with tool management:

- **Lost tools**: Equipment disappears across job sites without clear trails.
- **No responsibility tracking**: When tools change hands, accountability becomes unclear.
- **Manual sign-out sheets**: Paper forms get lost, damaged, or skipped entirely.
- **Scattered kits**: Accessories and components get separated from their parent tools.
- **Confusion on job sites**: No one knows what's available, what's missing, or who had it last.

Modern tool tracking solves these problems with QR scanning, automatic custody logs, and real-time location updates.

## What to Look For in Tool Tracking Software

- **How tools are identified.** QR codes and barcodes cost little and work with any phone camera. Bluetooth and GPS tags show where a tool is between scans, but cost more per tool and need batteries.
- **Check-out to people, trucks and sites.** Every hand-off should record who took the tool and where it went.
- **Kits.** A drill, its batteries and its case should go out and come back as one unit, with missing pieces flagged.
- **Consumables.** Blades, bits and fasteners need a count with low-stock alerts, not a record per item.
- **Reservations.** Specialty tools shared between crews need bookings that block double-booking.
- **Service reminders.** Inspections and calibration dates should come up before they are missed.
- **A phone app crews will use.** Scanning has to be faster than writing on a sign-out sheet.
- **Pricing per crew size.** Per-user pricing grows with every technician who needs to scan.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is tool tracking software?

Tool tracking software records which tools a company owns, who has each one and where it is, and logs every hand-off between people, trucks and job sites. Most systems identify tools with QR codes or barcodes scanned by phone; some add Bluetooth or GPS tags for live location.

### How do you keep track of tools on job sites?

Give every tool a label with a QR code or barcode, and scan it whenever it changes hands or moves to another site or truck. The software then shows who has each tool and where it was last scanned, so missing tools can be traced and returned.

### Does Shelf track tools with GPS?

Not with GPS hardware. In Shelf each tool has a location such as a site, truck or storage room, and a scan can record where the phone was when the tool was scanned. If you need a tool's live position between scans, you need Bluetooth or GPS tags from a tracking hardware vendor.

### How much does tool tracking software cost?

Pricing models differ: per user, per tool or one flat price. Shelf is free for one user with unlimited tools, and the Team plan is $67 a month or $370 a year for unlimited users.

### Can I track consumables like blades and drill bits?

Yes. In Shelf, consumables are tracked by quantity: one record holds the count, with a unit of measure and a low-stock alert, while each reusable tool keeps its own QR code. See [consumables tracking](/features/consumables-tracking).
