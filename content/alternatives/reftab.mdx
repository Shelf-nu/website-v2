# Reftab Alternative

Reftab is a cloud-based IT asset management platform. It covers hardware and software asset management: check-in and checkout, loan history, reservations, kits, asset audits, maintenance and work orders, request approvals, and integrations with tools such as Intune, Jamf, Jira, Microsoft 365 and Okta. Teams that compare it with Shelf are usually weighing four things Shelf does differently: open-source code you can self-host, flat pricing with no asset quota, separate workspaces per department, and kit checks on return.

Everything on this page about Reftab comes from Reftab's own website, checked on 27 October 2026. Where we are not sure, we say so.

---

## Overview: Reftab vs Shelf

Reftab's free plan tracks up to 50 assets with no time limit. The paid Hardware Asset Management plan is listed at $125 per month with unlimited users, and the Complete ITAM bundle is custom-priced "based on assets + software users". Reftab's pricing FAQ refers to a "billable asset quota", so the price moves with the number of assets you track. Reftab is proprietary SaaS: there is no self-hosted option and the source code is not public.

Shelf focuses on what happens when equipment moves between people. It tracks custody chains, reservations, and kit completeness, and every interaction (check-out, return, location update) can happen through a QR scan on any smartphone. Shelf is open source with unlimited assets and unlimited users on every plan.

## Where Shelf Takes a Different Approach

### 1. Flat Pricing With No Asset Quota

Reftab's free plan stops at 50 assets. Beyond that, the price is tied to how many assets you track: the ITAM bundle is priced "based on assets + software users", and the pricing FAQ talks about a billable asset quota. That is a normal model for IT asset management, where each asset is a laptop worth tracking on its own.

Shelf does not cap assets or users on any plan. Whether you track 200 items or 20,000, the price stays the same. For teams whose inventory is thousands of cables, lenses, tools and batteries rather than a few hundred laptops, that difference decides the bill.

See: [Pricing](/pricing)

---

### 2. Open Source and Self-Hosting

Reftab is proprietary SaaS. The source code is not available, there is no self-hosted option, and your data lives on Reftab's infrastructure. For most teams that is fine. For organizations with data sovereignty requirements, air-gapped sites, or a policy of inspectable code, it is a hard constraint.

Shelf is [open source](/solutions/open-source-asset-management) with a fully public codebase. Teams can read every line, self-host on their own infrastructure, or use Shelf's managed cloud. There is no vendor lock-in, and teams with development resources can extend Shelf to connect with any system their organization uses.

---

### 3. Multi-Department Workspaces

Reftab runs one account per organization, structured with unlimited locations and sub-locations and controlled with access roles. That works well for one IT team managing one estate. When IT, facilities, a media department and a teaching lab each want their own inventory, their own members and their own settings, the scoping has to be built out of roles and locations.

Shelf uses [workspaces](/features/workspaces) to give each team a fully separate inventory. Each workspace has its own assets, users, bookings and settings, and a person can belong to several. Nobody sees another department's equipment unless they are invited into that workspace.

See: [Workspaces](/features/workspaces)

### 4. Kit Checks on Return

Both products have kits. Reftab added kit templates in its January 2020 release notes: a template defines the components (a quantity from a category, specific assets, accessories), the app shows how many complete kits you can build, and scanning items fills in the checklist. Kits are checked out and returned as a unit, and items inside a kit can be returned separately. Reftab's own documentation says that reserving a kit template is limited to admins.

Shelf's [kits](/features/kits) are built the other way round: a kit is a fixed set of named assets that is booked, checked out and returned together by anyone with the right role. When a kit comes back, Shelf flags which components are missing before the next person picks it up. If your pain is the slow loss of chargers and lens caps from shared kits, that return check is the feature to compare.

See: [Kits](/features/kits)

### 5. Bookings: Both Have Them, Built for Different Rooms

Reftab's reservation system is mature. Users reserve assets, accessories and licenses for a date range, reserved items are blocked from checkout for those dates, and admins can set buffer times between reservations, business hours per location, how far ahead people may book, maximum reserved items per user, recurring bookings, and approval flows for requests. It is designed for an IT help desk or an equipment room with staff who fulfil reservations.

Shelf's [booking system](/features/bookings) is built for the people who use the gear. Any member with the right role books from a live availability calendar, Shelf prevents overlapping bookings automatically, and the booking carries straight into check-out and return on a phone, kit included. There is no separate request-then-fulfil step unless you want one.

See: [Bookings](/features/bookings)

![Bookings overview showing reservation calendar](https://qliecghuzfchfjwaisyx.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/website-images/features/bookings.jpg)

### 6. Custody History

Reftab keeps a loan history for every asset (who it was loaned to, when it went out, when it came back, who returned it, notes) and a separate changelog that records field changes with old and new values. Both can be reported on and exported.

Shelf keeps a [custody chain](/features/custody) for every asset as well, with each check-out, return and transfer stamped with the person, time and location. The difference is less in what is stored than in how it is written: in Shelf the transfer happens by scanning the asset's QR code on a phone, so the record is created by the person holding the item, not by an admin at a desk later.

## When Teams Choose Shelf Instead of Reftab

- **The inventory is large and cheap per item**: Thousands of cables, tools, props or batteries make per-asset pricing expensive, and Shelf has no asset quota
- **Open source is a requirement**: Data sovereignty, self-hosting, or a policy of inspectable code
- **Several departments share one platform**: Each team wants its own workspace, members and settings rather than one shared account scoped by roles
- **Kits must come back complete**: Return verification that flags missing components is the daily problem
- **Everyone books and scans**: Students, crew or field staff book and check out themselves from a phone, with no fulfilment desk in between

---

## When Reftab May Be a Better Fit

- **IT asset management end to end**: Reftab covers hardware lifecycle, software licences and SaaS usage in one product. Shelf tracks physical assets and does not do software asset management
- **Device-management integrations**: Intune, Jamf, Jira, Okta and 30+ others sync device and user data into Reftab. Shelf's integration path is its open-source codebase and CSV import/export
- **Maintenance and work orders**: Reftab's paid plan includes maintenance and work orders. Shelf has no maintenance module
- **Request approvals**: Reftab's request portal with approval flows suits help desks that gate equipment behind a sign-off
- **Managed-only simplicity**: Teams that never want to think about hosting get everything run by Reftab

Both platforms track physical assets, but they optimize for different realities. Reftab is built for IT teams that want one system for hardware, software and the help-desk workflow around them. Shelf is built for teams where equipment circulates daily and the people doing the check-outs are not administrators.
