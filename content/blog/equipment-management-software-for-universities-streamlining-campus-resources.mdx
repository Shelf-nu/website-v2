Equipment checkout and asset tracking software for universities is a shared system that records every piece of loanable or departmental equipment on campus, lets students and staff reserve it, logs who has each item and when it is due back, and lets staff verify what is actually on the shelf.

This page explains who on campus uses that kind of system, how it works in [Shelf](/), what three universities do with it, what it costs, how to move from Cheqroom or WebCheckout, and what Shelf does not do.

## Who uses it on campus

Most universities do not have one equipment problem. They have six or seven, each owned by a different department:

- **Media centers, film and art schools.** Cameras, lenses, audio recorders, lighting and grip gear that students book for coursework and productions.
- **IT loaner desks.** Laptops, chargers, hotspots and tablets lent to students and faculty, often for a day or a week.
- **Libraries.** Device lending alongside the book collection: laptops, calculators, cameras, VR headsets.
- **Labs and makerspaces.** Instruments, measurement tools, 3D printers and the supplies that get used up, like filament.
- **Athletics.** Team kit, training equipment and electronics that travel to away events.
- **Facilities.** Tools, AV carts, event furniture and anything that moves between buildings.

Most start on a spreadsheet, a paper sign-out sheet or a library system built for books, and break the same way: nobody knows who had the item last, two classes reserve the same camera, and a kit comes back without its batteries.

## How it works on a campus with Shelf

![University TV studio with tracked equipment](https://qliecghuzfchfjwaisyx.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/website-images/solutions/education-tvstudio.jpg)

### A QR label on every item

Every asset and every kit in Shelf gets its own QR code. Print the labels, stick them on, and any phone can open the item's page by scanning it. Gear that already carries barcodes can keep them (see [switching](#switching-from-cheqroom-or-webcheckout) below).

### Students request, staff hand over

Shelf has four fixed [user roles](/knowledge-base/user-roles-and-their-permissions), and they map neatly onto a campus:

- **Base** is for students and occasional borrowers. They browse the gear that is available for booking and click **Request reservation**. They cannot check anything out themselves, so nothing leaves until a staff member hands it over.
- **Self-service** is for trusted users such as staff, faculty or teaching assistants who should book and check gear out and back in for themselves, without waiting for the desk.
- **Administrator** is for the people who run the desk. They manage inventory, people and every booking, and they do the hand-over for Base users.
- **Owner** is the one account that owns the workspace and its subscription.

If you want hand-overs to be explicit, the workspace owner can require staff to scan or select each item at checkout and check-in rather than clicking one button for the whole booking. See [Require Explicit Check-in and Check-out](/knowledge-base/require-explicit-check-in-and-check-out).

### Bookings that cannot double-book

A [booking](/features/bookings) reserves items for a date range. While an item is reserved or checked out, it is unavailable to any overlapping booking, so two classes cannot hold the same camera for the same afternoon. [Working hours](/knowledge-base/working-hours-set-operating-schedules-for-your-workspace) stop people booking a pickup or return for a time the desk is closed, with holiday overrides for breaks.

For fleets of identical gear, such as 30 loaner laptops, you can group the units under an asset model and reserve "4x laptop" without picking serial numbers in advance. Whichever four units the desk scans at pickup fulfil the booking.

### Kits go out and come back as one

A [kit](/features/kits) groups items that always travel together, like a camera body, two lenses, batteries and a charger, under one QR code. The kit is booked and checked out as a single unit, so a student cannot take the body and leave the batteries off the record.

### Overdue items and who has what

When a booking's end time passes without a check-in, Shelf marks it **Overdue** and emails the people on the booking. Reminders go out one hour before pickup and one hour before the return is due. [Custody](/features/custody) shows who holds each item right now, including long-term loans that are not tied to a booking, such as a laptop assigned to a faculty member for the year.

Staff and students can also subscribe to a read-only [calendar feed](/knowledge-base/subscribe-your-calendar-to-shelf-bookings) of bookings in Google Calendar, Outlook or Apple Calendar.

### Audits at the end of term

With the [Audits](/features/audits) add-on, a staff member or student worker walks the room and scans labels. Shelf compares what was scanned against what should be there and flags found, missing and unexpected items.

### Supplies and reports

Makerspace filament, batteries and lab consumables can be [tracked by quantity](/features/consumables-tracking) with a low-stock threshold instead of one record per unit. Built-in [reports](/features/reports) such as Idle Assets, Asset Utilization, Top Booked Assets and Overdue Items show department heads what sits unused and who is late.

### A separate workspace for each department

Departments that want their own inventory, people and rules can each run their own [workspace](/features/workspaces). Workspaces are hard walls: the film school never sees IT's laptops, and nobody in one department sees another department's people or bookings. Each Team workspace is its own subscription, and owning more than two workspaces is arranged with our team.

### Campus sign-in with SSO

The SSO add-on supports single sign-on over SAML 2.0 with Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace, Okta, Shibboleth and other SAML 2.0 providers, with user provisioning included. You map identity-provider groups to Shelf roles, so a student group can land as Base and a staff group as Self-service or Administrator. See [SSO with Google Workspace](/knowledge-base/sso-with-google-workspace) for the setup.

### Web and phone

The full platform runs in any browser. The free Shelf Companion app on [iPhone and Android](/mobile-app) handles the desk work in the field: scanning, booking check-out and check-in, custody and audits, on the same workspace and login.

## Real examples from universities

![Mobile QR scanning for educational equipment check-out](https://qliecghuzfchfjwaisyx.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/website-images/solutions/phone-qr-alt-2.png)

### Kansas City Art Institute: media center checkout, moved from Cheqroom

KCAI's Media Center lends equipment to students for creative projects and moved from Cheqroom to Shelf. Their labels were printed on a DYMO LabelWriter 550, a workflow they wanted to keep. When their DYMO-generated QR codes were not picked up by Shelf's in-app scanner during booking creation, Shelf updated the scanner to read externally generated codes, so the Media Center kept its printer and label workflow and continued lending through the transition. [Read the KCAI case study](/case-studies/kcai).

### Eastern Michigan University: theatre and production inventory

EMU's Theatre & Entertainment Design & Technology Program had relied on spreadsheets, memory and rough location maps for its props and scenic elements, with student workers turning over every year as they graduated. With Shelf, every item is assigned a location, and students can search and book inventory remotely, so a student living in another state can find and reserve props for a show before arriving on site. The program reports removing hours of "blind digging" from production prep. [Read the EMU case study](/case-studies/eastern-michigan-university).

### Purdue University: drone fleets across three programs

Purdue's School of Aviation and Transportation Technology (SATT) replaced LibraryCat, a library system that only allowed 24-hour holds, so students could book aircraft days or weeks ahead. SATT logged 632 bookings in its first four months, with 289 in September as the fall semester started. Drones go out as kits with their associated gear, and maintenance checkouts plus the activity log keep a paper trail for FAA-regulated aircraft. Three Purdue programs now run independent workspaces with nearly 100 users, and the Institute for Digital Forestry uses self-checkout so faculty and staff do not need a dedicated equipment manager. [Read the Purdue case study](/case-studies/purdue-university).

## Switching from Cheqroom or WebCheckout

Two things make a move straightforward:

- **CSV import.** Export your assets from the old system and bring them in with the [CSV import](/knowledge-base/importing-assets-to-shelf-csv-guide), which is included on Plus, Team and Enterprise. Categories, locations, kits and custodians named in the file are created automatically.
- **Keep your labels.** The [Alternative Barcodes](/knowledge-base/alternative-barcodes) add-on reads Code 128, Code 39, EAN-13, Data Matrix and externally generated QR codes, so you can attach the barcode already on each item instead of relabelling the whole room.

**Coming from Cheqroom.** Cheqroom is a capable system and does several things Shelf does not: maintenance scheduling and service history, PDF agreements with digital signatures at checkout, GPS tracking on higher tiers, and Zapier integrations with thousands of apps. Like Shelf, it handles kits and bookings with overdue tracking, and its plans include unlimited assets and regular users. The main differences are pricing and structure. Cheqroom charges per administrator, so cost grows with the number of staff who run the desk, while Shelf's Team plan is one flat price per workspace. Cheqroom runs a single workspace per subscription, while Shelf lets each department run its own. Shelf is also open source (AGPL), so you can inspect the code or self-host. Full comparison: [Cheqroom alternative](/alternatives/cheqroom).

**Coming from WebCheckout.** WebCheckout is built around a staffed checkout desk and goes deeper into academic systems than Shelf: recurring reservations tied to course sections, patron rules by enrollment, and campus card and SIS integration. Shelf is lighter. Any authorized person can scan a QR label with a phone, booking is built into the plan, and new student workers can learn it in minutes. Full comparison: [WebCheckout alternative](/alternatives/webcheckout).

## What Shelf does not do

- **No payments or fines.** Shelf records overdue and damaged items, but it does not charge late fees, take deposits or collect payments.
- **No recurring reservations.** There is no automatic weekly series. Duplicate an existing booking and change its dates, which keeps the same equipment list.
- **No electronic signatures.** For a signed record, print the [check-in receipt](/knowledge-base/print-a-check-in-receipt-for-a-booking). It lists what came back, when and who received it, and has signature lines for signing on paper.
- **No LMS or SIS integration.** Shelf does not connect to Canvas, Blackboard, Moodle, Banner or other student information systems, so course enrolment does not change booking rights automatically. Access is set by role and workspace, and SSO group mapping can assign roles from your identity provider.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can students book equipment themselves?

Yes. Give students the Base role and they can browse bookable equipment and request a reservation from the web or the Shelf Companion app. They cannot check gear out themselves; a staff Administrator hands it over. Give trusted students or teaching assistants the Self-service role if they should check their own bookings out and back in.

### How do we stop double-bookings in a media center?

Put the equipment on bookings. Once an item is reserved or checked out it is unavailable to any overlapping booking, so the same camera cannot be held twice. Kits book as one unit, and working hours stop pickups and returns from being scheduled when the desk is closed.

### Can we use our existing barcode labels?

Yes, with the Alternative Barcodes add-on. It supports Code 128, Code 39, EAN-13, Data Matrix and externally generated QR codes, so you can attach the code already on each item instead of relabelling.

### Does Shelf support SSO for campus accounts?

Yes, through the SSO add-on: SAML 2.0 with Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace, Okta, Shibboleth and other SAML 2.0 providers, with user provisioning included. Identity-provider groups can map to Shelf roles. SSO is set up together with our team on a paid plan.

### How much does it cost for a university?

The Team plan is $67 a month or $370 a year with unlimited users. Audits, Alternative Barcodes and SSO are optional add-ons. Registered non-profit schools and universities get 10% off the Team plan, all add-ons and SSO on annual billing, and annual plans can be paid by invoice with a PO. Large or multi-site institutions can ask about Enterprise.

### Is there an accessibility VPAT?

Yes. Shelf maintains a VPAT documenting conformance with Section 508 and WCAG 2.1. Request it from the [education page](/industries/education).

### Can each department have its own inventory?

Yes. Give each department its own workspace. Workspaces are fully separated: assets, people, roles and bookings in one are invisible to another. Purdue runs three programs as three independent workspaces.

### Is Shelf a good Cheqroom alternative for universities?

For many campuses, yes. Shelf costs one flat price per workspace instead of per administrator, lets each department run its own workspace, and is open source. Cheqroom is the better fit if you need maintenance scheduling, digital signatures on checkout, GPS tracking or broad Zapier automation. Kansas City Art Institute moved its media center from Cheqroom to Shelf. See the full [Cheqroom comparison](/alternatives/cheqroom).
