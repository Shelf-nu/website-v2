An email address means the same person however it is typed. Shelf now treats it that way throughout the invite flow.

## The bug people actually hit

The [CSV user import](/knowledge-base/inviting-users-via-csv-upload) stored an address exactly as the spreadsheet had it, capitals and all — `First.Last@company.com`. Accounts are stored in lower case. So when the invited person opened their link while signed in, Shelf compared the two, found them different, and refused with **"Wrong user"**. Opening the link signed out gave an error page rather than joining, for anyone who already had a Shelf account.

The single "Invite a user" dialog already lowercased the address, so only invites made by CSV import carried capitals. That is a large share of the invites big teams send.

## What changed

The import stores addresses in lower case, trimmed of stray spaces. And every place an invite email is compared — the member check, the earlier-invite lookup, the pending-invite check, the account found on acceptance, cancel, and resend — now ignores letter case.

Three side effects go with it:

- A new invite for someone whose earlier invite carried capitals **reuses their existing team member** instead of creating a second one, so the person keeps the record that holds their history.
- A CSV that lists **one address twice in different capitals** creates one invite, not two.
- A CSV row for someone **already in the workspace** under different capitals is skipped, rather than inviting them again.

A re-send now closes every earlier invite for that person **before** it issues the new one. The two steps used to run at the same time, so a slow invalidation could close the invite that had just been created.

## Nothing to do on your side

Invites already stored with capital letters work as they are. There is no data change, no backfill, and nothing to re-send. If someone was stuck on "Wrong user", ask them to open their existing link again.

The team member's **name** still comes from the address exactly as you typed it in the CSV, because that name becomes the first name of the account created when the person accepts.
