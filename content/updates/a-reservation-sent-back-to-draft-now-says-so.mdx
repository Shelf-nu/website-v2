**Revert to Draft** is how a reservation request is handed back for changes rather than refused: the booking is not cancelled and nothing on it is lost, it just becomes editable again so dates, items or the custodian can be adjusted and the booking reserved a second time.

Until now it told nobody. A Base or Self-service user pressed **Request reservation**, an administrator sent the booking back, and the requester learned about it the next time they happened to open the booking. Every other transition already emailed — reserved, cancelled, checked in, extended, deleted — and this was the one gap in the set.

## What changed

Reverting a reserved booking now sends the same fan-out as the rest of the lifecycle. The subject reads *"↩️ Booking reverted to draft"* with the booking's name, and the body says the recipient can review the booking, make changes and submit it again.

Who receives it follows the rules already in **Settings → Bookings**: the custodian always, the booking's creator while **Notify booking creator** is on, everyone on the always-notify list, and anyone added as a recipient on that booking. The all-admins broadcast is not part of it — that one is the signal that a *new* request needs attention, and this event is the answer to a request rather than a new one.

If the email cannot be sent, the revert still stands. The booking's status is written first, so a mail problem leaves you with a booking that is back in draft, not a transition that half happened.
